Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Retrieval-Augmented Generation, or RAG, is an architecture that combines a retrieval
system with a generative language model. Instead of relying solely on knowledge baked
into the model's parameters during training, a RAG system first retrieves relevant
documents or passages from an external knowledge source, then conditions the language
model's generation on that retrieved context.

This approach addresses two well-known weaknesses of large language models: outdated
knowledge and hallucination. Because the retrieved context is injected directly into
the prompt at inference time, the model can produce answers grounded in current,
verifiable information rather than purely from memorized training data, and it can cite
its sources.

A typical RAG pipeline has three stages. First, documents are split into chunks and
embedded into vectors, then stored in a vector index. Second, at query time, the user's
question is embedded and used to retrieve the most relevant chunks. Third, the
retrieved chunks are combined with the question into a prompt and passed to a language
model to generate the final answer.

Advanced RAG systems add refinements at every stage: smarter chunking that respects
document structure, hybrid retrieval that blends keyword and semantic search, reranking
models that reorder candidates by relevance, query rewriting or expansion to handle
ambiguous questions, and contextual compression that trims retrieved passages down to
only the sentences that matter before they reach the model.
