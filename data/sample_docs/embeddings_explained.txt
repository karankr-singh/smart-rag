Text Embeddings

A text embedding is a dense vector of real numbers that represents the meaning of a
piece of text in a way that machine learning models can process. Embeddings are learned
such that texts with similar meaning end up close together in the vector space, while
unrelated texts end up far apart, regardless of whether they share the same words.

Early embedding techniques, like TF-IDF, represented text as sparse vectors based on
word frequency, capturing only lexical overlap. Latent Semantic Analysis improved on
this by applying dimensionality reduction, such as singular value decomposition, to
uncover latent topics that group co-occurring words together, giving a cheap, fully
local approximation of semantic similarity.

Modern neural embedding models, such as those built on transformer architectures, are
trained on massive text corpora using contrastive objectives, pulling semantically
similar pairs closer together and pushing dissimilar pairs apart. These models capture
much richer semantic and even syntactic relationships than earlier bag-of-words methods.

The quality of embeddings directly determines retrieval quality in a RAG system: if the
embedding model fails to place a relevant passage near the query in vector space, no
amount of downstream reranking or prompting can recover the information that was never
retrieved in the first place.
